AI-Augmented Business Analysis: Comparison & Evaluation Report (Task 5)
Author: Sonia Mannepuli

Overview:
This report compares the findings from a manual data analysis approach with the 
insights generated by an AI assistant using the provided e-commerce datasets.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Key Similarities & Differences
   - Similarities: Both manual and AI analyses accurately identified the February revenue 
     peak and the status of Electronics as the highest revenue-per-order category.
   - Differences: Manual analysis was largely descriptive (reporting raw numbers), 
     whereas the AI analysis was prescriptive (proposing marketing and operational actions).

2. Accuracy of AI Outputs
   - The AI output was highly accurate, correctly interpreting the percentage growth 
     and identifying the positive correlation between customer orders and total spending.
   - All statistical claims made by the AI aligned with the calculations derived from the datasets.

3. Missed or Incorrect Insights
   - Manual Analysis Missed: The human analyst failed to identify the "quick win" of 
     cross-selling Electronics to Clothing shoppers, which the AI suggested.
   - AI Limitations: The AI suggested dropping the "Books" category without considering 
     long-term strategic brand value or potential future demand, which would require 
     further human oversight.

4. Practical Usefulness of AI in Analytics
   - Speed: AI significantly accelerated the initial exploratory phase, allowing for 
     instant metric generation and pattern recognition.
   - Strategic Augmentation: AI is most useful as a "sparring partner," providing 
     initial hypotheses that a human analyst can then validate against business context.
   - Conclusion: AI is a powerful tool for scaling data synthesis, but it does not 
     replace the need for human business judgment, especially when making decisions 
     that impact inventory or brand strategy.
